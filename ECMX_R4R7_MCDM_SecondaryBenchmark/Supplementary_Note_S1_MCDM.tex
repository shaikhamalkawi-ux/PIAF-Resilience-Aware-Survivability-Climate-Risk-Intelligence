\section*{S1. Purpose and claim boundary}
This supplementary benchmark addresses the reproducibility and all-site-comparability concern raised during peer review. It does not replace the main manuscript's Pareto screen and is not presented as independent physical validation. The same ten fixed benchmark locations and the same two decision dimensions used by the primary screen are retained: mean global horizontal irradiance (GHI), for which higher values are preferred, and the historical climate-context summary $C$, for which lower values are preferred. No location, climate input, Pareto calculation, or bootstrap result is changed.

The baseline split $w_R=w_C=0.5$ is a declared diagnostic reference convention. It is not a calibrated physical weighting and it is not an elicited stakeholder preference. A deterministic full-range sensitivity over $\lambda\in[0,1]$ is therefore reported with $w_R=\lambda$ and $w_C=1-\lambda$.

\section*{S2. Common decision matrix}
For the cross-method common-matrix benchmark, the two criteria are converted to benefit orientation on $[0,1]$ across the fixed ten-site benchmark:
\begin{equation}
r_l=\frac{R_l-R_{\min}}{R_{\max}-R_{\min}},\qquad
c_l=\frac{C_{\max}-C_l}{C_{\max}-C_{\min}}.
\end{equation}
Here $R_l$ is the mean-GHI resource quantity from the main manuscript, $C_l$ is the historical climate-context summary, and $\mathbf{y}_l=(r_l,c_l)$ is the secondary benefit-oriented decision vector. This shared min--max representation is used to make WSM, TOPSIS, and VIKOR operate on one declared matrix. A conventional vector-normalized TOPSIS audit is reported separately below because classical TOPSIS is commonly formulated with vector normalization \cite{hwang1981,opricovic2004}.

\section*{S3. WSM}
The weighted-sum score is
\begin{equation}
A_l=\sum_j w_j y_{lj},
\end{equation}
with larger values preferred.

\section*{S4. TOPSIS}
For the shared-matrix TOPSIS benchmark,
\begin{equation}
v_{lj}=w_jy_{lj}.
\end{equation}
With positive and negative ideal vectors $\mathbf{v}^+$ and $\mathbf{v}^-$, the distances are
\begin{equation}
D_l^+=\sqrt{\sum_j (v_{lj}-v_j^+)^2},\qquad
D_l^-=\sqrt{\sum_j (v_{lj}-v_j^-)^2},
\end{equation}
and the closeness coefficient is
\begin{equation}
CC_l=\frac{D_l^-}{D_l^+ + D_l^-},
\end{equation}
with larger values preferred \cite{hwang1981}.

\section*{S5. VIKOR}
For benefit-oriented criteria, define the normalized loss
\begin{equation}
d_{lj}=\frac{f_j^*-y_{lj}}{f_j^*-f_j^-},
\end{equation}
where $f_j^*$ and $f_j^-$ are the best and worst observed values of criterion $j$. The group utility and individual regret are
\begin{equation}
S_l=\sum_j w_jd_{lj},\qquad
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{VIKOR}}_l=\max_j(w_jd_{lj}).
\end{equation}
The compromise statistic is
\begin{equation}
Q_l=v\frac{S_l-S^*}{S^- -S^*}+(1-v)
\frac{\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{VIKOR}}_l-\mathcal{R}^*}{\mathcal{R}^- -\mathcal{R}^*},
\end{equation}
with $v=0.5$ and smaller $Q_l$ preferred \cite{opricovic2004}.

For $m=10$ alternatives, the acceptable-advantage threshold is $DQ=1/(m-1)=1/9=0.1111$. Condition C1 requires $Q(A_2)-Q(A_1)\ge DQ$. Condition C2 requires $A_1$ also to be best according to $S$ or $\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{VIKOR}}$. The implemented compromise rule follows the standard three-branch interpretation: if C1 and C2 both hold, $A_1$ is reported; if C1 holds but C2 fails, $A_1$ and $A_2$ are reported; if C1 fails, all leading alternatives satisfying $Q(A_M)-Q(A_1)<DQ$ are retained.

\section*{S8. Normalization audit}
The common decision matrix uses shared min--max normalization so that all three methods operate on identical dimensionless inputs. Because classical TOPSIS is commonly stated with vector normalization \cite{hwang1981,opricovic2004}, a conventional vector-normalized TOPSIS audit was also run. At $w_R=w_C=0.5$, it retains Antofagasta first and Aswan second. Its rank-one switch occurs between $\lambda=0.732$ and $0.733$ on the same 0.001 grid, compared with the shared-min--max TOPSIS switch between $0.534$ and $0.535$. The leading pair is therefore stable at the declared equal-weight reference, but the precise scalar switch is normalization-dependent.

\section*{S9. Five-criterion structural diagnostic}
As a secondary structural diagnostic only, the climate summary was represented by its four already reported normalized components, with total resource weight 0.5 and weights 0.125 for heat, humidity, GHI variability, and wind exposure. Under that representation, all three methods rank Aswan first and Antofagasta second. The change in scalar winner arises from the representation and normalization geometry, not from new evidence. This diagnostic is not used to alter the primary Pareto result.

\section*{S10. Reproducibility}
The executable script, input table, complete CSV outputs, workbook, and SHA-256 manifest are provided in the R4R7 secondary-benchmark reproducibility folder. The input table is the same locked ten-site table used by the main R4 analysis.
